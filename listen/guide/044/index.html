Thirty-nine. The Turn on the Half-Landing

Listen

Synthetic narration

The wardrobe crosses the half-landing differently in memory and in the present. Thirty-one years earlier, father and son carried it upward sideways and caught a wrist at the turn. Now it lifts an inch and drops back. Removing its door makes no difference. It can remain at a charge to the estate or be broken up at cost. After the keys are returned, it stands doorless awaiting the estate charge and a photograph with a measuring rule.

The continuation once gave the room a usable presence. He learned to hear her before looking, argued with her about money and his brother, and stayed another hour even after his hand closed on the cold table. At lapse, a standard voice spoke four sentences. The small humming host disappeared. For eleven months he returned each second Sunday to dust its empty shelf. The physical absence does not undo the earlier conversation; tending the shelf does not restore its host.

Smaller objects leave through different acts of selection. The license, receipts and certificate go into a carrier bag. Two plates are set aside without a decision and later found in his bag. His father's coat is carried out. The hardship schedule becomes a place to write the wardrobe's cost, and its fold catches biscuit crumbs. Deliberate, unbidden and practical uses coexist while the wardrobe remains.
