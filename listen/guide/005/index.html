Three. The Grace Period

Listen

Synthetic narration · Download audio

Several clocks appear together without becoming one reliable chronology. The wall grants twelve hours from the first breath. The mother is eleven hours in; the desk man arrives at half past four. A word is spoken at eleven hours forty, and the account opens at four fifty-two. The closing statement gives eleven hours of age and eight minutes of registration. The guide preserves those statements rather than calculating a corrected birth time.

Three helpers work beside the registration process. The midwife offers tea, a clean cloth, prayer knots and her witness signature. Meral keeps the printer near the empty tag, although her workload request remains unsigned. The desk man explains the charges while his screen requires an occupied bed to be admitted again. Registration brings material provisions, but the witness's name and the note about the grace period remain attached to the account.

The private word and the registered name are separate acts. The mother speaks the word twice and reserves it for later. The short name is entered, the account opens and the tag closes around the ankle. The child holds a word for which nobody has been paid. No entry for that word is shown; its future cannot be certified from this scene.
