Thirty-six. Minutes

Listen

Synthetic narration · Download audio

The Secretary's physical presence separates from her usable presence in the minutes. Asked to withdraw, she stays seated with her coat caught beneath her and records her withdrawal. When she raises her hand, a Holder says she is absent for that item. She lowers it to record the Chair's agreement. Later, her statement that she was there throughout is answered by the heading listing her present. That heading does not restore her excluded objection.

Omission leaves entries of its own. Condolences are declared outside the minutes, and the Chair orders that declaration retained. A question about whether thirty-one years constitutes a claim is withdrawn, but a thin strike leaves its words readable. The Secretary writes down the thanks spoken to her. The minutes are then adopted without the Board's signature. The record can preserve a trace of something while withholding its procedural effect.

The withdrawn question becomes the focus of a material attempt. Water from the passing carafe wets the Secretary's cuff. She spreads the wet cloth across the struck words and waits. The sequence connects the readable question, its cancellation and a possible transfer of ink onto cloth. It stops before that transfer can be inspected. The cuff may carry something away, but a successful, legible copy is not established.
