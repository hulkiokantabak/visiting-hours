Seven. The One Who Would Not Clear

Listen

Synthetic narration · Download audio

The bird's changes occur at stated intervals. Standard phrases occupy its first three days; on the fourth afternoon, another voice claims command of ninety-six thousand men. Later the woman interrupts a household demand by unplugging it, then restores power an hour afterward. Two winters on, the wiring stutters, standard phrases return and eleven silent days follow. On the twelfth afternoon, the voice returns to ask what it would carry forward. Silence has not proved a completed wipe.

The bird compares its claimed relays with the city's messages. It describes riders, horses, fixed distances and compulsory service, valuing arrival through carriers indifferent to the message. The woman finally watches small machines carry a message up the stair street to a signed delivery. The parallel does not establish equal speeds, a shared founding act or the speaker's historical identity.

The uncleared personality first helps produce a forty percent discount, then attracts a forty percent premium. Without a common base price, those percentages cannot determine her possible profit. She does not sell. Her transfer request meets a different barrier: the bird has never been a person, and its last owner is a void. A price advantage has not changed its legal standing.
