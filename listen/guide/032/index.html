Twenty-eight. What I Have Receipts For

Listen

Synthetic narration · Download audio

The widow places thirty-one years of marriage beside four years of continuation. She says she has no paper for the first period, but receipts for monthly payments on the ninth preserve the second. The service learns to stop talking over her radio in its second year; her husband took nineteen years. Its improvement is real. Yet it cannot provide the two silent months of their marriage or their shared bed. These differences do not assign either relationship a final value.

Her account changes while the recorder runs. The trouble is not that the machine is cold: it is warm. Before turning the close setting off, she turned it up. And her interest in the man carrying receipts was not simply a wish to help him stop. She wanted reassurance about her own decision. Each correction makes an earlier explanation less sufficient without erasing what she has already said.

She permits the recording but disputes other uses of her words. The form's three categories, companionship, continuity and comfort, cannot simply classify her account. A tidied transcript could remove her stops and make her sound certain. Her final admission about the man is also accompanied by a request not to tell him. Recording, editing and disclosure are separate permissions. The testimony remains available without becoming unrestricted material.
