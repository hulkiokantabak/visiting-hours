Synthetic narration

The corrector's decisions vary with each plate and stone. A fish trader's epitaph is filed as corresponding; a wife's independent wording becomes family-supplied sentiment. A silent plate sits above a promise of Thursday visits. Nine paid crossings prompt two marks, though the tablet accepts one. Taken from us faces the stone's taken, with nothing filed. A warning about the one underneath is entered as corresponding despite predating the plate by eleven years. Granite is not automatically certified as the truer witness.

Her mother's memorial keeps another disagreement. The sentence she remembers buying says her mother made every room easier; the renewal says warmer. The granite remains blank six years later. She avoids the row and files a category meant for a locked gate. Monthly payment continues without resolving the two wordings, or explaining how they diverged.

Her workload is numerically clearer: six hundred and twenty-nine examined, three hundred and eleven remaining, nine hundred and forty altogether. She finds one hundred and six discrepancies; the office average of two hundred and eighty has no stated comparison denominator. Idris, the shared flask and the remembered neighbor's soup bring company. Taking the lettering card opens a possibility without showing an inscription, a payment or an end to the debit.
