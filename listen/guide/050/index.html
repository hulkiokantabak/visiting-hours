Forty-four. Press Nine

Listen

Synthetic narration

The caller presses three to report a fault, but the late laugh conforms to the licensed profile. Zero offers someone to speak to, then reports that he has left the queue. Four reaches estates, where the counterparty takes no calls. Five asks for the name. Two offers the voice; nine repeats the options. These are his selected steps, not the service's complete menu.

The call is free, yet contact remains tightly limited. A recorded apology counts as contact, and the voice sample is available once per calendar year. What he hears is breath before the first word. The sample has now been used. The menu can repeat, but the sample cannot simply follow it. This consumes his annual sample, not a whole year of his life.

The quiet-box also holds memory in its surfaces. His knees know the stool's position. Varnish covers old initials on the door. A key remains in his pocket, and repeated pressing has worn the number from nine. Beyond the glass, a man waits. These marks suggest familiarity and repetition without supplying an exact duration or identifying the waiting man's role.
