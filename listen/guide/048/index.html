Forty-two. Errata

Listen

Synthetic narration · Download audio

The composing service repeatedly changes the form of agreement. The widow crosses out the claim that her husband's agreement is retained: he was already dying. The claim becomes that he would have agreed. She objects again and asks to ask him. Her own words, I asked for him, are then placed beneath the renewal. These are proposed corrections inside this story, not changes to the events in other stories.

At the kitchen table, the connected voice says no when she requests no, and yes when she requests yes. Neither answer establishes her dead husband's wishes. A remembered breath draws her attention. She asks to hear it again, then disputes where her words have been placed. The clerk offers to disconnect the speaker. She signs and asks for lower volume.

The physical copy and the filed account diverge. A hand needs no alteration, but its action can be reassigned to instructions. Removing the damp mark enlarges it. The required signature is recast as corrections requested by the widow. She keeps the book uncorrected while a signed request for a corrected edition remains on file. The filing does not certify freely given consent.
