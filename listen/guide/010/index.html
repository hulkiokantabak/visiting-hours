Eight. Final Position

Listen

Synthetic narration · Download audio

A promised extension and a loan record disagree with what the man encounters. The fitter stamps the hand's service as extended, but his phone rejects the stamp. The narration calls the paperback his copy, lent before the last adjustment; the loan slip identifies him as the borrower. Both versions remain visible. The guide neither corrects the slip nor decides that the fitter knowingly lies. Her continued attempts do not prevent the midnight shutdown.

Other records hold different parts of the hand's history. The flesh hand was lost at forty-two; the replacement has reported grip, tremor and temperature for eleven years. An exact total of nights cannot be derived. The stopped opening of jars for two leaves the household loss unidentified. An open final position requires a technician; a closed one needs no further visit. No perpetual visiting charge is stated.

On the final evening he buttons, unbuttons, peels an orange and signs with both hands. He chooses closed, then puts his living hand inside the mechanical one. The last charge closes its fingers; he stays until morning. A later letter gives that choice an administrative value: a small credit. His use of the field exceeds its service calculation without defeating the shutdown.
