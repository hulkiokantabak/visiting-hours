Twenty-two. Kindly Confirm

Listen

Synthetic narration

On the first Thursday, forty mothers exchange folders. Forty renewals follow, and no invoices go out. Over the weekend the register puts itself right. By Monday the mothers' saved printouts agree with the files rather than with Thursday. A second signature box then appears on the form. The next protest changes its action: sisters join the mothers, a shoe holds the entrance open, and the ticket machine crosses the threshold. The outcome of this Second Thursday is not reported.

The same hall handles two problems differently. A payment taken twice in April is refunded before the mother asks. Yet her photographs, all retaining the same notch across her son's eyebrow, do not resolve his paused renewal. The refund slip is real; she holds it up to the queue. At school his name is still read, and somebody answers. Neither correction explains the other case.

The mother also contests descriptions of the protest. She makes the bearer of a replacement companion read the paused line beneath its claim of victory, then refuses permission to copy her son's file. She does accept childcare from the organizer, specifying when the boy eats before taking another ticket. Publicity and practical help are separate choices. At the later protest, the guard stamps on the shoe holding the door.
