Thirty-two. A Place Is Held

Listen

Synthetic narration

The invitation describes a mother eleven hours into her son's life, bleeding and still holding him. A desk worker says the fee would double at twelve hours. With the hardship schedule on her blanket, she supplies a name; the midwife witnesses it, and an account opens for a cot, warmth and a reading at the desk's convenience. The leaflet calls the first twelve hours free and records their use as repeatable kindness. Payment of a doubled fee is not shown.

Its selected testimonials are true but incomplete. The widow says the service was very good and corrected her sister's birthday. Her fuller account also admits preferring the continuation on some evenings while missing the marriage's two silent months. The man's difficult second year and four hundred extra mornings remain true in his incompatible accounts. The invitation selects comfort from statements whose remaining words complicate its use. Recovering those words does not make the comfort false.

Each offer has its own boundary. The first Thursday and assessment are free. An introductory rate lasts four quarters. A place will still be held in ten years, without a charge for holding. Neither a defined bed nor free future continuation is promised. The final two blanks ask whose voice is wanted and who will pay. Desire and payment occupy separate fields, and the reader is not required to call the offered voice the same person.
