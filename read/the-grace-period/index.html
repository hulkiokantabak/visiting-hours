The Grace Period

Story 3

Listen

Synthetic narration

The license desk on the maternity floor does not close. Cold at the ankles, heat at the ceiling. The women stand with their arms crossed.

THE GRACE PERIOD, says the wall. TWELVE HOURS FROM THE FIRST BREATH, DURING WHICH NO CHARGE ACCRUES.

In the private wing a woman names hers in four minutes. The tag clips. The account opens. Cot, warmth, one reading a day. She chooses Brave New World. Her sister asks if she remembers what happens in it.

“I remember the voice,” she says.

She settles back when the reading starts. At the other end, a ramp nobody here is thinking about.

The woman in the third bed is eleven hours in. Her son has no name. Blood comes away in clots. Her stitches pull when she lifts him. She has not put him down.

The midwife brings tea twice. The second time she says it: unnamed at twelve hours goes to pending.

"My brother's pending. He's forty-one. He's never stood in a queue. He's never been served in one either."

The midwife presses a thumb into the prayer knots in her pocket. Her grandmother tied them. She holds the last knot until she can face the bed again, then brings a clean cloth.

At half past four the desk man comes up. He explains, correctly and at no charge. The fee doubles at twelve hours, again at a week. He prints the hardship schedule. Leaves it on the blanket. He does not look at her clock.

His screen lists the bed as empty. Meral from care liaison looks from it to the woman.

“You'll have to admit her,” he says.

“Again?”

“For the bed to exist.”

Meral squares the hardship sheet with the locker. Her request for two floors instead of three is still unsigned downstairs.

“Leave the printer.”

He says it has to go back. She sets it on the locker. When he reaches for it, she puts the empty tag beside it.

“When there's a name on this.”

At eleven hours forty the mother says a word into the baby's ear. The midwife has heard nineteen years of first words. This is not a name.

"Is that the name?"

"No. That one's for later."

She says it again. The whole word.

"And the name?"

She looks at the wall. "Write it down."

The name is short. The midwife has never heard it in this city. She signs as witness. Her name beside his account for as long as it exists.

The account opens at 04:52. Cot. Warmth. A reading, at the desk's convenience. The tag clicks around his ankle.

The fourth line is a note: registered at eleven hours forty. Not removable. It goes with him to school, to the licensing hall, to the ramp. The grace period is free. The use of it is recorded.

He is eleven hours old and eight minutes registered and he has a word in him that nobody has been paid for.

At six the midwife carries down the linen. The third bed's is heavy. She stops at the window nobody cleans. Does the arithmetic she does every night.
