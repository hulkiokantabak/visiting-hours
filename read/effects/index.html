Effects

story eighteen

Listen · 8:04

The office of effects sits at the water end of the customs house, three rooms and a wall of shallow trays, and the man who keeps it has kept it thirty years. His signature closes a dozen files a day and has never once felt like his; it sits on the line like a guest on the edge of a made bed. The water gives up what it gives up — the drowned, the fallen, the found — and what is in their pockets comes here, to be dried, laid out, and read. He is good at the reading. A life leaves a residue in its pockets the way a night leaves salt on a boat, and he has learned the residues: which fold in a ticket means a man who checked it four times, which wear on a coin means a widow who could not spend it. The office identifies effects, and through the effects, the dead, and through the dead, the estates, and the estates wake the renewals, and the renewals wake everything else. On the far side of the identification stands the dusk boat. The registered dead board it within the week, their fares standing from the old accounts. The unidentified go to the northern wall, which does not face the water.

There is a junior now, sent down from the licensing hall, who reads the rule-book in the slow afternoons as other young men read the sporting pages. The junior is the one who finds him the tray.

The tray is ordinary until it is not. A watch, the kind you wind, stopped at a quarter past something, one initial on the case. A tram ticket folded to the width of a nail, folded and refolded, the fold of a man deciding not to spend. A pocket dictionary, swollen to twice its life, dog-eared at a page the water has taken. A bone button. A length of green thread wound around a matchstick. He reads the tray as he has read ten thousand trays, and the tray reads him back.

The watch is his father’s watch. The initial is the initial he polishes with his cuff on the tram without thinking. The fold in the ticket is his fold. He puts his thumb to the reader to open the effects register, as he does forty times a day, and the screen holds him and returns a sentence he has only ever seen printed on other people’s paper: pending confirmation of identity.

The register has matched the tray. The card prints while he stands there. The name on the card is his name, whole, and the entry it wakes is his entry — the renewals, the desk fee of a schooling forty years done, the standing orders, the small correct life of a documented man. The body downstairs, says the card, is him. The office is asked to confirm.

He goes down and looks through the glass and the face is a water face, past reading. In the glass, over the dead face, his own face stands where the light puts it, and for the length of a breath he cannot tell which of the two expressions he is wearing. But the hands he knows. He stopped having a brother the winter he turned six. He remembers the registry office that winter, the shape of his father’s hand around his, the cold of the counter at eye height, and coming home fewer. Nobody in the house said struck, then or after. The word was for other families. What he remembers is that there had been two of most things — two bowls, two coats on one hook — and then there was one of everything, and the watch, which had been promised to the firstborn, stayed in the drawer for years before it came to him as if it had cooled.

The brother lived, then. Lived the way the unregistered live, which is to say beneath the reading — no renewals, no entries, no queue he could stand in, no rate he could save toward. Nothing to identify. A man made entirely of effects. And at the end he went to the water, the crossing that is not sold, and the water did what the water does, and the tray came here, to this office, to this desk, to the one man in the city who could read it.

The junior, at his shoulder, reads the rule aloud, proud of knowing where it is: identification is by effects; the officer’s signature completes it; the estate follows the name. And beneath it, in the old type the amendments never touch: A name can cross once.

So the office holds one name and two brothers, and the name is good for one crossing, and it has always been good for one. There is a box on the form for the identifying officer’s own retention, which the office reads as a declaration of interest and prices at four words a year, for as long as the file is open. If he signs, the identification stands: the body is the name, the name boards the dusk boat with its fare paid from the old account, and his brother crosses — crosses as him, in his place, spending the only eternity the family was issued. And the man at the desk becomes what his brother was: a discrepancy, a warm error, pending forever. If he voids the match, the tray is nobody, the body is nobody, the northern wall takes another number, and the name stays his — the renewals, the desk, the documented life, and, at the end of it, the boat. Stays his, flagged. The match has been seen; voiding does not unsee it; a file that has once said pending keeps a thin gray margin forever, and renewals read margins.

He thinks about it for two days, which the office allows; the water is patient and the trays do not spoil. He winds the watch each morning and it keeps the time it kept. He tries the dictionary once, on the second evening. The water has fused it to one wet stone of words. It opens nowhere. He does not force it.

On the third morning he signs. The reader wants the signature against the specimen, as it always does, and the file brings up the specimen card, taken the registry winter, a name in a child’s careful hand. He looks at it. It is careful, and the care is a child’s with something that was never loose in his hand — each letter placed, not made. He has been signing that name for fifty years the way you sing a song learned through a wall. The hand on the specimen card is not his hand. It was never his hand. The dead man’s fingers downstairs, water-ruined, are the fingers that made it.

The record had not erred. It had waited. The registry winter the family went in with two boys and a strike to place, and the name went where the name went, and the child who came home was the one who came home. The office asks him to confirm the identity of the deceased and he confirms it, in his own hand, which no file in the city has ever held. His hand does not shake, and he notices the not-shaking, a silence where a tremor should be. It is the signature of a man who does not exist, accepted by a reader that files it under the name it can find.

The dusk boat takes the name across the water entire. Effects travel with the crossing dead, all but one item, which the office retains against error. He retains the dictionary, swollen shut, its dog-eared page gone to the water, and enters it: RETAINED. Under REMARKS he writes nothing. The watch goes with the boat, wound, keeping the time it kept, on the wrist it was promised to.

The office light stays on over the trays. At the desk the man winds a watch he no longer has, the wrist remembering the weight. The water brings what it brings.
